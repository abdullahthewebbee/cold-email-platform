SPF, DKIM, and DMARC Explained for Cold Emailers

By Devlet Technology · January 2026 · DevOutreach Blog

SPF, DKIM, and DMARC are the three email authentication standards that decide whether mailbox providers trust your emails. Get them right and you reach the inbox; get them wrong and you land in spam. Here's what each one does, in plain English.

What is SPF?

SPF (Sender Policy Framework) is a DNS record that lists which mail servers are allowed to send email on behalf of your domain. When an email arrives, the receiving server checks whether it came from an authorized server. If not, that's a red flag.

Think of SPF as a guest list: only the servers you've named are allowed to send as you.

What is DKIM?

DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail) adds a cryptographic signature to every email you send. The receiving server uses a public key published in your DNS to verify the signature. If it matches, the email genuinely came from your domain and wasn't tampered with in transit.

Think of DKIM as a tamper-proof seal: it proves the message is authentic and unaltered.

What is DMARC?

DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance) ties SPF and DKIM together. It tells receiving servers what to do when a message fails authentication — do nothing, quarantine it, or reject it — and can send you reports on who is sending as your domain.

Think of DMARC as the policy: it decides the consequences when the guest list or the seal doesn't check out.

Setting them up correctly

For cold email, aim for a DMARC policy of at least p=quarantine — this tells providers to treat unauthenticated mail as suspicious rather than delivering it. A policy of p=none provides reporting but no protection. Make sure both SPF and DKIM pass before tightening DMARC, or you risk blocking your own legitimate mail.

The bottom line

SPF says who can send, DKIM proves the message is genuine, and DMARC sets the policy when checks fail. All three are non-negotiable for cold email in 2026. Verify them before your first send — and re-check whenever deliverability dips.
